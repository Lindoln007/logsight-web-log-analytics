## Design Context

### Users
LogSight is a final-year project: a web-based analytics system for uploaded Apache/Nginx access logs. Its most important audience is **examiners and supervisors watching a demonstration**, often on a projector or shared screen. They are technically literate but have never seen the product, are not log specialists, and judge it in minutes. The person driving the demo needs every screen to explain itself: what dataset is open, what was analysed, what was found, and what to look at next.

Job to be done: upload a log file, understand the traffic it contains, and see what the three AI modules (forecasting, anomaly detection, security classification) concluded — without anyone having to narrate the interface.

### Brand Personality
**Exact, annotated, unhurried.** The product should feel like a well-typeset scientific report that happens to be interactive: findings are written as sentences beside the evidence, numbers are set in tabular figures, and nothing shouts. Emotional goals: confidence in the results, and the sense that the system has done the reading for you.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme: light only.** Chosen for projectors and for screenshots that go into a printed report. No dark mode and no theme toggle.
- **Tone: precise and editorial.** Serif display type for headings and written findings, a classic editorial gothic for interface text, a highly legible mono for log lines.
- **Type:** STIX Two Text (display and findings prose — the typeface of scientific publishing), Libre Franklin (interface — the newspaper gothic that traditionally sits beside a text serif, with clean tabular figures), Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono (log lines and paths — its slashed zero and distinct letterforms suit raw data).
- **Colour:** cool paper-white surfaces tinted slightly toward the brand hue; near-black ink for text and primary actions; one accent, a cobalt "annotation ink" blue, used sparingly for the active location, links and the model's marks on charts. Status colours (vermilion, ochre, green) are reserved for data meaning and always paired with a word or icon.
- **Structure over decoration:** hairline rules, generous margins, numbered sections and captions instead of boxes inside boxes. Cards only where an item is genuinely a separate object.
- **Anti-references:** generic SaaS dashboards (rounded cards with drop shadows in a grid, big-number hero tiles, purple/indigo gradients), glassmorphism, dark "hacker" monitoring consoles, Tailwind-default slate-and-indigo.

### Design Principles
1. **Say the finding, then show the evidence.** Every analytical screen opens with a plain-language conclusion; charts and tables support it.
2. **One dataset, always in view.** The working dataset is chosen once and shown in the frame of every page; no screen asks the user to pick it again.
3. **Legible from the back of the room.** Large type for conclusions, strong contrast, tabular figures, and never colour alone to carry meaning.
4. **Rules, not boxes.** Use whitespace, hairlines and typographic hierarchy to group content; reserve containers for things that are truly separate.
5. **Motion explains.** Transitions show where content came from and that work is in progress; nothing moves for decoration, and reduced-motion preferences are honoured.
